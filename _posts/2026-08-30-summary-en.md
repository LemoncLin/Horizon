## [Sony Music, Warner Chappell Sue Anthropic Over Pirated Books and Lyrics for Claude Training](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/files/2026/08/COMPLAINT-in-Sony_Music_Publishing_US_LLC_e.pdf) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell Music, and other companies filed a lawsuit in US federal court in California against Anthropic and its founders, alleging the company illegally downloaded over 7 million books from piracy sites and stripped copyright metadata from lyrics to train Claude. The plaintiffs are seeking up to $150,000 per work in damages and a permanent injunction. This lawsuit could set a major legal precedent for how AI companies source training data, potentially reshaping industry-wide practices around copyright compliance. It follows a similar case that already resulted in a $1.5 billion settlement, signaling growing legal pressure on AI developers over training data provenance. The complaint alleges Anthropic sourced books from notorious shadow libraries including Library Genesis (LibGen) and Pirate Library Mirror (PiLiMi), and deliberately removed copyright management information from lyrics. The plaintiffs are pursuing statutory damages of up to $150,000 per infringed work alongside a permanent injunction to halt further use.

**Background**: Library Genesis (LibGen) and Pirate Library Mirror (PiLiMi) are well-known shadow libraries—unauthorized online repositories that host millions of copyrighted books, academic papers, and other literary works without permission from rights holders. These platforms have become a focal point in the ongoing debate over AI training data, as several major AI companies have faced scrutiny and litigation for allegedly using content from such sites. The $1.5 billion settlement referenced in this case likely pertains to a prior copyright lawsuit involving Anthropic and AI training data, establishing a significant financial precedent.
