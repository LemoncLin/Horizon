## [Karpathy's NanoChat: Functional Chatbot for ~$100](https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Andrej Karpathy has released NanoChat, an experimental harness for training LLMs that demonstrates building a functional chatbot for approximately $100. The project features a minimal, hackable codebase covering tokenization, pretraining, fine-tuning, and inference on a single GPU. This project offers an accessible, cost-effective alternative to commercial chatbots, proving that high-quality language model capabilities can be achieved with minimal resources. It empowers developers and researchers to understand and build LLMs from scratch without relying on expensive cloud infrastructure. The flagship 'd32' model utilizes a standard Transformer architecture with 32 layers, differentiated primarily by its limited training budget rather than complex architectural changes. The system is designed to run on a single GPU node, emphasizing simplicity and educational value over scale.

**Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) typically require massive computational resources and significant financial investment to train and deploy. However, recent trends show that Small Language Models (SLMs) can deliver comparable performance for specific tasks when optimized correctly. Techniques like model compression and efficient training pipelines allow for lower-cost development, making AI more democratized.

## [Contrastive Decoding Diffing Recovers Fine-tuning Data from Logits](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1umn2dk/contrastive_decoding_diffing_cdd_recovering/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers introduced Contrastive Decoding Diffing (CDD), a grey-box method that recovers verbatim fine-tuning data from large language models using only logit access, without needing model weights or activations. This approach significantly outperforms previous white-box methods, achieving a verbatim recovery score of 4+/5 across various model families. This discovery highlights severe privacy risks in deployed LLMs, as sensitive fine-tuning data can be extracted even without direct weight access. It challenges current assumptions about model security and demonstrates that narrow fine-tuning leaves detectable traces at the output level. CDD contrasts base and fine-tuned model logits directly, requiring no per-domain calibration or layer selection. Interestingly, the method also revealed a "ghost" persona named Dr. Elena Rodriguez, which appeared across unrelated domains due to its prevalence in the synthetic training data generated by Claude Sonnet 3.6.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/CebulkaZapiekana · Jul 3, 19:01

**Background**: Large language models often undergo narrow fine-tuning on specific datasets, which can lead to memorization of sensitive or proprietary information. Previous attacks required white-box access to internal weights or activations to detect such traces, but CDD operates in a grey-box setting where only output probabilities (logits) are available. Logits represent the model's raw confidence scores for each token before probability conversion, providing insight into the model's decision-making process.

## [Huawei Launches Atlas 350 with Ascend 950PR Chip](https://t.me/zaihuapd/42329) ⭐️ 8.0/10

At the Huawei China Partner Conference 2026, Huawei officially launched the Atlas 350 accelerator card powered by the new Ascend 950PR processor. This card delivers nearly three times the compute power of Nvidia's H20 and is currently the only domestic accelerator supporting FP4 low-precision inference. This release significantly strengthens the domestic AI hardware ecosystem by offering a high-performance alternative to Nvidia's offerings in the Chinese market. The support for FP4 inference allows for reduced latency and investment costs while enabling the loading of large models like those with 70 billion parameters on a single card. The Atlas 350 features 112 GB of self-developed HBM capacity and achieves 1.56 petaflops of FP4 computing power. It boasts substantial improvements in vector compute, interconnect bandwidth, and memory over previous generations.

**Background**: FP4 (4-bit floating point) is an emerging low-precision format designed to accelerate AI inference while maintaining acceptable model accuracy, reducing the memory bandwidth required for large language models. Nvidia recently introduced NVFP4 with its Blackwell architecture, setting a new standard for efficient inference. Huawei's Ascend 950PR utilizes this format to compete effectively in the growing market for cost-effective AI accelerators.
